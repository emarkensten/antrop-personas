  • Erkännande att hon redan bryr sig så mycket hon mäktar med.

● PRIMARY · DESIGN TARGET · NARRATIVE 02 / 10

Anna is up at 06:20 every weekday. Coffee, two lunchboxes, school drop-off, train, open standup. By 09:00 she has already made forty decisions, none of them about food that matters to her. Her own breakfast was a piece of toast eaten standing up. She doesn't quite remember it.

She used to read every label. There's a memory of standing in front of the chicken shelf in 2018, looking up Brazilian soy on her phone. Now she reaches for the same thing she reached for last week, because that decision has been pre-made. She still cares — that hasn't gone — but the cost of caring at the shelf has become the cost of caring everywhere, and everywhere is more than she has.

At home, dinner is the place where her values and her household collide. One child will not eat lentils. The other one might, but only if his sister does. Her partner cooks twice a week and defaults to what works. The result is a kitchen that does the same five dinners on rotation, and a low-grade guilt about it that she carries quietly.

She is open to change but not to friction. A meal-plan service that did the thinking for her — that priced realistically, that swapped one weekly meal at a time — would move her. A campaign that tells her she should care more would not. She is already caring as hard as she can afford to.

"Min mentala belastning är redan maxad. Att lägga till fler beslut är svårt."
